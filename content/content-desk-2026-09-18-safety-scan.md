# Friday Safety Decision Note — Shelby County, Texas
**Date:** Friday, September 18, 2026  
**Decision:** **A material safety post is warranted today.**

Shelby County has a **current outdoor-burning ban** and local disaster declaration effective September 17 because of wildfire risk from drought and extremely dry grassland fuel. The county’s official homepage says “BURN BAN IN EFFECT,” and the Texas A&M Forest Service’s current feed lists Shelby among 217 counties under burn bans. This is a practical, current community-safety reminder—not a reason for alarm.

## What is current—and what is not
The county homepage also shows a **September 9 notice that the prior burn ban was lifted**. That information is stale for today’s decision: it is superseded by the newer **September 17 “BURN BAN IN EFFECT”** notice and the reported September 17 order. The reported order applies to **outdoor burning in unincorporated Shelby County**, with a stated initial duration of no more than seven days unless continued or renewed by Commissioners Court. The Forest Service feed corroborates the ban, although it does not give the local scope or expiry.

No active NWS watch, warning, or advisory was found for Shelby County (TXZ153). The forecast calls for routine hot, mostly sunny weather near 96°F today; this alone is **not** a separate material-post trigger. The Forest Service incident viewer showed **Shelby 8608 contained (3.62 acres, 100% contained)**, not an active county wildfire or evacuation event. No separate evacuation order, broad road closure, flood emergency, or major severe-weather warning was confirmed in the sources reviewed.
