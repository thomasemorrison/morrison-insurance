# Morrison Insurance — Texas Roof-Age Rule Content Brief

**Prepared:** September 30, 2026  
**Status:** Published to the live Morrison website on September 30, 2026. No social post, Google Business Profile post, email, schedule, or paid-media change has been made.

## Bottom line

This is a **strong, useful local homeowner topic** if Morrison treats it as a plain-language question—not an election-cycle story and not a promise of lower premiums.

The accurate takeaway is:

> In Texas, a company cannot decline or nonrenew a home policy **solely because of a home’s or roof’s age**. It may still consider the **physical condition** of the roof and other parts of the home.

TDI’s September 29 news release describes the current regulatory work as a clarification. Its proposed amendment says directly that the edits **do not change the rule’s meaning or application**, do not expand the existing prohibition, and are not expected to increase or decrease the number of people covered by the rule. The proposal also says it has no measurable state or local fiscal impact and does not impose new compliance costs. [1] [2]

**Morrison should therefore not say:**

- “Texas just banned roof-age denials.”
- “An older roof now has to be insured.”
- “The rule means your premium will go down.”
- “A company cannot nonrenew you over your roof.”
- “This fixes Texas home-insurance prices.”

## What the official sources actually say

| Topic | Source-supported statement | Content limit |
|---|---|---|
| Age alone | TDI says companies cannot decline or nonrenew a home policy simply because a house or roof is older. [1] | Do not convert this into a guarantee of eligibility or renewal. |
| Physical condition | TDI says a company may base its decision on the physical condition of the home or components, including roofing, wiring, heating, air conditioning, plumbing, and siding. [1] | Do not diagnose a roof remotely or speculate about a company’s inspection. |
| Regulatory status | TDI’s proposal calls the amendments clarifications that do not change the existing rule’s meaning or application. Public comments are due November 9, 2026; a public hearing is scheduled for November 3, 2026. [2] | Use “TDI is clarifying an existing rule,” not “a new rule took effect.” Recheck before publication. |
| Cost claims | TDI’s proposed-rule analysis says the edits do not create a new regulation, expand or limit the existing regulation, or positively or adversely affect the Texas economy. [2] | Do not connect the clarification to a premium reduction or an affordability result. |
| Underwriting | TDI says underwriting considers a home’s age and condition along with replacement cost, construction, location, occupancy, fire protection, claims history, and other factors. It says companies cannot turn someone down just because of a home’s age or value, though they may charge more. [3] | Avoid saying every company assesses these factors in the same way. |

### Proposed article outline

1. **Start with the answer:** Age alone is not supposed to be the only reason for a decline or nonrenewal; condition can still matter.
2. **Age versus condition:** Use simple examples without diagnosing any roof—an older, maintained roof versus a roof with visible wear or unresolved damage.
3. **What a homeowner can gather:** renewal/nonrenewal notice, any inspection report or photo request, repair records, replacement details, prior claim information, and questions for an agent or carrier.
4. **What this does not mean:** no automatic approval, no coverage guarantee, no promise about price, no promise a particular roof condition will be accepted.
5. **A calm next step:** bring the notice and roof records to a local agent to talk through questions and compare options where appropriate.
6. **Source box and review date:** Cite TDI’s September 29 release and proposed amendment; identify the article as updated September 2026 and recheck TDI if the rulemaking moves.

## Social copy direction — do not publish yet

> **Does an older roof automatically mean a Texas home policy can be turned down?**
>
> Not by age alone. Texas insurance rules prohibit a company from making that decision solely because a home or roof is older. But a company can still consider the physical condition of the roof and other parts of the home.
>
> The useful question is not only “How old is my roof?” It is also “What condition is it in, and what information should I have ready?” A renewal notice, inspection details, repair records, and clear questions can help you understand what you are being asked for.
