# Wednesday Safety Decision Note — Shelby County, Texas
**Scan date:** September 17, 2026  
**Decision:** **Material safety post warranted today**  
**Material alert:** `true`

## Decision rationale
A concise community-safety post is warranted because **current local reporting says Shelby County Commissioners reinstated the burn ban on September 16 and that outdoor burning is once again prohibited**. Shelby County Today’s current homepage also labels the linked notice **“Burn Ban Active.”** This is a practical, countywide behavior change with clear public-safety relevance—not a fear-based emergency message.[^county-today]

The post should **not** imply an active wildfire, evacuation, or weather emergency. The Texas A&M Forest Service incident viewer showed no active Shelby-named incident in the reviewed results; its listed “Shelby 8608” fire was 3.62 acres and 100% contained.[^tfs-status]

## Important source distinction
**Current evidence supporting the post:** the September 16 Commissioners action and the currently displayed “Burn Ban Active” link from Shelby County Today.[^county-today]

**Stale or conflicting information not relied on by itself:** Shelby County’s official homepage still displays “BURN BAN IN EFFECT,” but that item is dated **August 5** and may be stale. Conversely, the Texas A&M Forest Service county burn-ban text feed, timestamped September 16, omits Shelby County. The supplied review also identifies internal inconsistency in one account of the September 16 meeting. Accordingly, the post should stick to the simple, reported prohibition on outdoor burning and should not add effective dates, exemptions, penalties, or enforcement details. Those details should be confirmed directly with Shelby County before any operational guidance is given.[^county-home][^tfs-ban][^county-today]

NWS conditions are hot and dry, but there are no active NWS watches, warnings, or advisories for zone TXZ153; heat conditions alone are not the basis for this post.[^nws-alerts][^nws-forecast]

Please help protect our community by holding off on outdoor burning and checking with Shelby County for any questions about current rules. Stay safe, Shelby County.
