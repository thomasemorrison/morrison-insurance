## Facts verified and how they are used

The Texas Department of Insurance's shopping checklist asks whether a policy covers how the car is used and whether exclusions apply. It also says not to cancel current insurance until the new policy or written proof of coverage is received. The article uses these as preparation questions and a calm timing reminder rather than a coverage promise. [1]

TDI's auto insurance guide identifies the declarations page as the policy summary of coverages, dollar limits, and deductibles. It advises asking who is covered and whether anyone is excluded. Some companies require everyone of driving age in the household on the policy. TDI describes business driving, ride-hailing, and paid delivery among uses most policies do not cover. The article explicitly qualifies this: specific use must be discussed and actual policy wording matters. It does not say every business/farm task is excluded or prescribe an insurance product. [2]

TDI's teen-driver page says to notify the company when a teen is learning in the insured vehicle. Its learner-permit distinction does not support a universal insurer enrollment rule. The article asks readers to report permit/license status and ask when that company needs the teen listed. It does not promise a claim outcome or say every permit holder must be added at the same time. [3]

The NAIC quote-preparation article recommends the current auto policy, licenses of all drivers, and vehicle registration. It says to include all drivers and describes driving-history questions. Its 2011 publication date means it is used only for durable document-preparation guidance, not current legal requirements, universal reporting periods, online platform behavior, or discounts. [4]

The NAIC declarations-page explainer verifies policy number, effective dates, vehicle details, VIN, lienholder, coverages, limits, and garaging information. It specifically cautions that drivers may or may not appear on the declarations page. The article therefore asks for any separate driver list and exclusion documents rather than saying the declarations page always lists every driver. This source is also from 2011 and is used for document anatomy, with TDI supplying Texas support. [5]

NHTSA offers a public VIN decoder for identifying information encoded in the VIN. Results are manufacturer-reported. The article does not describe the decoder as proof of ownership, insurance eligibility, or a substitute for retrieving the actual VIN. [6]

TDI's personal-auto review checklist verifies that a named-driver exclusion must identify each excluded driver and be accepted in writing by the named insured. This supports asking for written exclusions and which driver is named. The article does not market or imply availability of an obsolete named-driver-only policy. It uses “named drivers” in the ordinary driver-list sense and “specifically excluded by name” for the separate exclusion question. [7]

## Editorial and qualification notes

The five-item list is a practical starting checklist, not a universal list of mandatory documents. Names, dates of birth, license information, commute, mileage, vehicle storage, ownership, financing/leasing, and a desired start date are preparation prompts. Different insurers can ask for different information. Mark uncertain dates or numbers as missing or estimated instead of guessing. No universal accident/violation look-back period is asserted.

Retrieval suggestions—checking an insurer's app/account, asking the current company or agent for a declarations page, and using registration/title/purchase paperwork or asking a dealer for a VIN—are practical editorial guidance. They do not guarantee every provider has an app or a particular document-delivery workflow. The federal VIN link is verified separately. No unsupported dashboard-location assertion is included.

The warning never to post licenses, VINs, birth dates, policy numbers, or document photos in social comments is practical editorial privacy guidance, not a claimed statutory requirement or a claim that VINs are secret. The article advises using a known agency contact and asking for the preferred private sharing method. It does not claim Morrison Insurance operates a secure upload portal, guarantee a channel's security, or treat social direct messages as a preferred document channel.

TDI's general shopping-tips page was also fetched. Its retrieved coverage-amount wording appeared inconsistent with the 30/60/25 explanation in the consumer auto guide. No minimum-limit numbers from it were reused. [8]

The FTC privacy feature page was fetched, but it is a broad directory, not evidence for a specific legal rule on posting licenses or VINs. It is not cited in the public article. [9]
