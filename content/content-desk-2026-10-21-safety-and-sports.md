## Safety decision — active burn ban and elevated fire danger

Shelby County’s October 15 order prohibits outdoor burning in the county’s unincorporated areas. The order is a local disaster declaration that can run for no more than seven days unless the Commissioners Court continues or renews it. The Texas A&M Forest Service’s October 20 list also includes Shelby County. [1] [2]

The National Weather Service has no active watch, warning, or advisory for the Center point. It does, however, keep a **Fire Danger Statement** in effect through **7 PM Friday, October 23** for East Texas, including Shelby County. The statement cites very dry fuels, afternoon relative humidity in the upper teens to near 30%, and east to northeast winds near 10 mph. This is elevated fire weather, not a Red Flag Warning for Shelby County. [3] [4]

**Decision:** This remains useful as a brief, factual reminder. It should not say there is a new burn-ban order or imply an evacuation or active wildfire. A fresh county check is required before reusing the copy after the order’s current seven-day window.
