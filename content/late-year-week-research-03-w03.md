## Week anchors and editorial guardrails

This is the week before Thanksgiving. The federal holiday is **Thursday, November 26, 2026**, so Thanksgiving itself is not inside the requested November 16–22 window.[1] The useful editorial frame is preparation rather than a holiday announcement: make a neighbor-help plan, check the home and outdoor utility before colder weather, verify current outdoor rules, and share practical local resources without implying a local drive, event, closure, forecast, or office schedule.

Shelby County’s TPWD page identifies Center as the county seat and lists the 2026–27 county regulations. During this week, the page shows North Zone white-tailed deer general season (November 7, 2026–January 3, 2027), North Zone regular duck season (November 14–29, 2026), East Texas squirrel season (October 1, 2026–February 28, 2027), statewide quail season (November 1, 2026–February 28, 2027), and other species-specific rules.[2] These are useful anchors for an outdoor-context post, but they must not become a hunting instruction, legal conclusion, or generalized “season is open” statement. The current county page and applicable species page must be rechecked before publication.

Texas Department of Insurance guidance supports a plain-language home-and-utility check: remove hoses, wrap exposed pipes, drain or turn off irrigation where appropriate, store lawn equipment, locate and test the water shutoff, clean gutters, trim trees over homes and cars, and test smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms.[3][4] These are general Texas preparedness points, not a local freeze forecast. Do not say a freeze is coming unless a current NWS forecast supports that claim.

Shelby County Outreach Ministries says its mission is to feed hungry people and assist people in need in Shelby County; its site lists the Outreach Center, Senior Nutrition Site, Unique Boutique, and Meals On Wheels.[5] Its Meals On Wheels page says hot meals are delivered every weekday to seniors in the Center area and asks for volunteer drivers and financial contributions.[6] Because program details and needs can change, a post may point neighbors to the organization’s current information, but it must not invent a Thanksgiving meal, donation drive, shortage, or Morrison sponsorship.

The current NWS active-alert feed for Shelby County is the required source for a warning-led replacement. At research time it showed no active alerts, but that is only a snapshot and cannot be carried forward to November 16–22.[7] The county’s public-notice page is the required source for burn-ban orders. Search results showed a possible October 23, 2026 order, but the fetched public-notice page did not expose the order text; therefore no burn-ban status is used as a claim below.[8] If an outdoor-burning topic is considered, the live county order must be read and its effective date, scope, and supersession status confirmed; the Texas A&M Forest Service list may corroborate but cannot replace the county order.[9]
