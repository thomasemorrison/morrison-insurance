# Weekly website-to-lead plan — Morrison Insurance

Starting **Monday, November 16**, the existing Content Desk will prepare one focused website improvement each week and report what happened to genuine local inquiries. Monday chooses the work; Friday closes the loop in the same handoff. The weekday 6:30 AM Central schedule, this Morrison-only thread, and the sports, safety, fishing and Rural Ag lanes remain in place. No additional recurring task or email has been created.

The priority is **more qualified local inquiries from the website**. More relevant visitors and better engagement help, but they are not the finish line. A neighbor who quickly finds the number and calls can be a good outcome even without a long visit. The plan will not inflate events, add filler articles or treat every click as a lead.

## Count real conversations first

A genuine inquiry is an actual insurance request received by Morrison, not spam, a test, a vendor message or a job application. Report qualified local inquiries separately: the agency confirms that the person or insured risk is in the area Morrison serves and that the request is relevant. Separate new-business inquiries from routine existing-customer service. A browser's location is not enough to qualify a lead. [5]

A form followed by a phone call about the same request counts once. Deduplicate in the agency's private lead register or CRM, not in public reports. Keep the weekly handoff anonymous. Record website origin only when it is confirmed or reported; otherwise leave it unknown. Do not put names, emails, phone numbers, policy details or free-text messages into Analytics, campaign links or this project's scorecards. [5] [6]

Successful forms, phone-link clicks and directions clicks are supporting measures. A phone click does not prove a connected call. A persisted form can still be spam or unqualified. The measurement reference distinguishes those signals and proposed event names; nothing has been installed or made a conversion goal by this plan. [5] [7]

### The weekly scorecard

Each Monday's handoff will report the following, with dates and data gaps beside the numbers:

- **Primary outcome:** genuine website inquiries and the qualified local subset, shown as actual counts. Keep confirmed and reported website origin distinguishable.
- **Contact path:** accepted website forms, telephone-link clicks, and agency-confirmed website-related calls. Do not add them together as though they were different people.
- **Relevant traffic:** website sessions by source and landing page, with Google organic traffic separate from social, paid, direct and referral traffic. Do not confuse new-user acquisition with session acquisition.
- **Search opportunity:** query/page clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position for the selected local topic. Show the actual landing-page mapping when supplied.
- **Supporting engagement:** homepage and priority landing-page engagement, bounce and contact activity. These help identify friction but do not replace the inquiry count. [2] [5] [8]

Use a completed seven-day view for operating activity and comparable completed 28-day windows for trends. Show absolute change and denominators alongside percentages. If the prior lead count is zero, percentage growth is undefined. Small counts need a caution, not a dramatic success claim. Overlapping rolling reports show direction but cannot prove that a site change caused it. [5]

When calculating a form-success session rate, divide sessions containing a verified success by the same eligible measured sessions. Do not divide CRM inquiries by unrelated users, GSC clicks or pageviews and call it a conversion rate. Only calculate a genuine website inquiry rate when attribution and the denominator are compatible. After a tracking repair, establish a new consistently measured baseline: more recorded events can mean better measurement rather than more real business.

**Initial targets:** every genuine inquiry gets an origin/qualification review in the agency's private record; every week's selected improvement has an owner, one primary measure and a verified release status; avoidable contact-path defects are addressed first. Set an agreed numerical lead-growth target after the first reliable baseline. The desired direction is more qualified local inquiries and relevant organic visits—not a guaranteed percentage increase from today's incomplete data.

## The same weekly cycle, not a new content machine

**Monday: measure and choose one improvement.** Pull the dated reports and the anonymized agency lead count. Review the prior approved change. Select the best evidenced measurement defect, visitor-path problem or high-intent local search opportunity. Produce the exact proposed change and why it should help someone reach the right local person.

**After approval: build, test and verify.** Follow the GitHub-first workflow. For a page refresh, keep its URL unless there is an approved reason to change it; update the relevant metadata, dates, internal links and feed/sitemap records truthfully. Test the actual contact route on a phone and desktop, with keyboard access. A form test must use an approved appropriate method or a genuine inquiry—not an unapproved fabricated lead. Verify the real public release on both Morrison domains; a commit or checkpoint alone is not deployment. [4]

**Support the useful page, without changing the community mix.** Prepare channel-specific education that answers the same local question and points to the verified page. Add a consistent campaign label only to future approved external links; do not overwrite existing approved schedules. Never add UTMs to internal site links or claim query-level lead attribution from GSC. Check fresh Metricool counts before assigning a social date. The limit stays two placements per channel per day, with 7–10 useful posts weekly each on Facebook and Instagram, Thursday fishing room reserved, and lighter GBP/LinkedIn planning. [8] [9]

**Friday: record what actually happened.** In the existing Rural Ag/day's handoff, record approval and public-release state, available inquiry signals and any unresolved access issue. If a change was not approved or released, it was not a completed experiment. Carry one next decision into Monday. A low-volume experiment can stay under observation for several weeks; a new weekly brief does not mean rewriting the same page every seven days.

The automated part is research, the scorecard and review-ready preparation. This request does not authorize automatic ordinary social publication, website release, tracking changes, synthetic submissions, paid-media changes or deployment-setting changes. Existing narrow NWS and Thursday fishing permissions remain unchanged.

## First four Monday cycles

### November 16 — make website inquiries measurable

First review the current HubSpot form and the existing tag path read-only. The October 26 diagnostic recorded a possible mismatch between the embedded form's completion and a Google Ads tag dependent on `/thank-you`; it did not prove that submissions or Analytics are broken. Check the currently installed form/editor, actual confirmation behavior and agency receipt evidence before proposing a repair. Do not change Ads goals under a website analytics task. [10]

For an updated-editor HubSpot form, the documented success signal is `hs-form-event:on-submission:success`; legacy forms use a different callback. The implementation must match the actual form, count success once and avoid forwarding personal fields. An ordinary thank-you pageview or submit-button click is not a reliable substitute. This is a specification for a separately approved repair, not installed tracking. [5] [7]

**Monday deliverable:** a short tracking acceptance brief, the first dated scorecard or clearly marked data gap, and the exact approval needed if a defect is found. A successful submission should reconcile with an accepted record; phone intent should remain separate from an actual inquiry. Prepare the homepage test below in parallel, but make only one primary visitor-path change at a time.

### November 23 — clarify the homepage's existing call action

The homepage already offers a direct call link, a visible phone number and “Speak with a Local Agent.” Contact already gives the office address, Monday–Friday 9 AM–4 PM hours and an explanation of what happens after contact. The useful hypothesis is not “add a call button.” It is that visitors may hesitate because the hours and first-conversation expectations are not beside the homepage call action. This is an observation from current public page text, not a proven conversion defect. [11]

**Proposed supporting copy**, subject to agency confirmation of hours and wording:

> Monday–Friday, 9 AM–4 PM · 125 Shelbyville St., on the Center Square. When you call, a local agent can help you talk through your situation and the questions to ask next.

Place a short, readable version beside the existing call action. Keep the existing hero and buttons rather than adding competing forms, popups or a chatbot. Include no response-time guarantee, price promise or invented testimonial. Check actual mobile placement and contrast before release.

**Primary measure:** genuine website-origin inquiries, with phone clicks as a secondary signal. Compare consistently measured before/after windows and note changes in traffic mix. Do not declare a winner from one additional click. Bring this change forward if measurement is already sound and agency approval is available; the dates are a work sequence, not a reason to delay a known repair.

### November 30 — choose one current near-ranking page

Use fresh GSC query/page exports to decide whether the November 9 quote guide is gaining relevant clicks or whether another existing local service/resource page has the better supported opportunity. Compare its displayed search title and description with the actual reader question. Improve one specific answer, search snippet or useful internal route—not every part of the page at once.

**Monday deliverable:** the chosen query and mapped page, its dated evidence, the exact change, a factual reviewer and one primary success measure. If fresh GSC data is still missing, do not reuse position 11.1 as today's ranking; select a separately observed contact-path issue instead. A new article is appropriate only for a verified separate need that existing content cannot satisfy.

### December 7 — retain what helps and fix the next bottleneck

Review the longest consistently measured window available. Keep a helpful change unless there is evidence of a usability problem; low counts alone do not establish that it failed. Inspect the next existing high-intent page or contact route only when its evidence supports attention. Possible follow-ups include making an auto-page button's contact-page destination clearer, or putting confirmed office hours beside a resource's stop-by invitation. These are hypotheses, not already-approved edits. [11]

**Monday deliverable:** what is live, what can be measured, what remains unknown, and one next improvement. Continue observing earlier releases as later work moves ahead. The cycle then repeats, with a monthly agency review of qualified inquiries and the questions customers are actually asking.
