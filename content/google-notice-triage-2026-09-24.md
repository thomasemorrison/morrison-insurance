## Attribution update — owner context received after initial review

The owner noted that the supplied notices **could relate to Moon Rabbit Exp rather than Morrison Insurance**. That is a credible explanation for the Customer ID discrepancy, but it remains unconfirmed because the screenshots do not establish the signed-in Google Ads account name, account hierarchy, or the Search Console property domain. The notices are therefore classified as **unassigned**. They must not be used as evidence for a Morrison paid-media or website change until direct, read-only account/property confirmation attributes each message to the correct business.

The first and controlling issue is an **unresolved Google Ads Customer ID mismatch**: the notices identify **133-171-2290**, while the prior audited/restoration work identifies **830-642-4851**. No evidence supplied establishes that these IDs are linked, unrelated, or otherwise represent the same Morrison account. Until the owner confirms the intended account through authenticated access, no recommendation may be mapped to the prior audit or acted on. The notices are not proof of a paid-media defect, a Customer Match authorization, a genuine strategist relationship, or an active sitewide outage. The supplied September 24 public checks did not reproduce a 5xx or true 404 on the live Morrison sitemap sample, but they did identify canonical and HTTP-200 fallback signals that warrant separate read-only validation only if the Search Console notice is confirmed to belong to Morrison.

## What is confirmed, what is not

**Confirmed from the supplied evidence:** the Customer IDs differ; no authenticated current Google Ads or Search Console review was available; earlier work recorded partial approved changes only in **830-642-4851**; the two strategist emails were not opened or contacted; and all described work was read-only. The live-site sample returned HTTP 200 for the sitemap and sampled pages, while the sampled canonical and nonexistent-path behavior described above was observed.

**Not confirmed:** whether either Customer ID is Morrison’s active account; whether the accounts have any relationship; whether Target ROAS is active or inappropriate; whether a Customer Match feature tier is available or lawful for Morrison’s intended use; whether the strategist emails are authentic or useful; whether the reported Search Console errors are current, intermittent, historical, or tied to other URLs; and whether Google selected the homepage canonical for the affected pages. A Googlebot-style user agent in a public test is not verified Googlebot traffic, and a point-in-time HTTP sample cannot rule out intermittent origin, CDN, rendering, geographic, or crawl-environment failures.

## Safe, sequenced next-action plan

1. **Confirm notice attribution and account identity first.** Have the authorized owner/admin independently determine whether each notice belongs to Morrison Insurance or Moon Rabbit Exp, then document the relevant business name, Google Ads Customer ID, Search Console property domain, and any visible manager relationship. Do not infer a relationship between **133-171-2290** and **830-642-4851** from the notices or prior audit.

2. **Collect read-only Ads evidence for the confirmed ID only.** Export or screenshot campaign inventory, bid strategy and target, business goals, primary conversion actions, conversion values, 30–90 days of cost/value performance, and conversion lag. Determine whether the notice’s recommendation actually applies to an active campaign. This step should not alter goals, conversions, budgets, bidding, assets, audiences, tags, billing, or campaigns.

3. **Keep Customer Match in a privacy review lane.** Treat enablement or availability as permission to consider a feature, not as an instruction to use it. Before any list is contemplated, obtain written owner approval and separate privacy/legal review of first-party provenance, notice/disclosure, consent, data subjects and geography, retention/freshness, permitted use, feature tier, and campaign implications.[2] [3]

4. **Verify outreach outside the email path.** Preserve both messages without interaction. If verification is later approved, capture sender metadata without clicking links. Use an independently navigated official Google support page, not the email, to decide whether any contact is appropriate. No strategist call should be booked at this stage.[6] [7]

5. **Resolve the Search Console evidence gap.** In confirmed read-only Search Console access, open the `Server error (5xx)` and `Not found (404)` issue details. Record exact examples and crawl timing, then run indexed-result review and live inspection on those exact URLs. A 404 may be correct if content was intentionally removed; a redirect should be considered only when there is a genuine, relevant replacement. Do not treat a current HTTP 200 alone as proof that a historical report is cleared.[4] [5]

6. **Correlate before proposing web remediation.** Compare exact reported URLs with origin/CDN logs and repeat compatible GET checks. Separately document the homepage-canonical and HTTP-200 fallback behavior. The web owner/developer may prepare a reversible proposed configuration change for review only after Search Console confirms the affected URLs and live findings.

## Changes that require separate approval

The following are **out of scope and not authorized by this report**: any Target ROAS or Target CPA adjustment; bid-strategy, budget, campaign, asset, business-goal, conversion-action, tag, billing, or audience change; Customer Match upload, list creation, activation, refresh, synchronization, or sharing of customer data; strategist call, meeting booking, reply, support-form submission, or access grant; and any Search Console validation, indexing request, sitemap/robots/canonical/redirect/error-handling change, hosting/CDN/firewall change, or web-content change.

A later proposal must identify the confirmed account/property, the exact setting or URL, the evidence supporting the action, expected impact and risks, owner, rollback method, privacy/legal sign-off where applicable, and explicit written approval. Until then, the decision is to preserve the current state and investigate through read-only review.
