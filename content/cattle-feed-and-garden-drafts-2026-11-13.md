## Cattle feed — start with the hay already on hand

The useful point is to check hay quality and cattle's needs before choosing supplemental feed. AgriLife's August 14, 2026 hay-testing guidance supports matching forage quality to cattle groups; its 2017 winter-preparation article supports differing needs and gradual supplement changes. Only general feeding guidance is used—not the older article's weather, prices or example ration amounts. No current Shelby County forage shortage or personalized feeding rate is claimed. [1] [2]

### Facebook caption

November is a good time for small-herd owners around Center and Shelby County to look at the hay already on hand before choosing supplemental feed.

Texas A&M AgriLife Extension recommends testing hay for protein and energy, then matching available hay and forage to cattle's needs. Age and stage matter: a cow nursing a calf has different needs from a dry cow. If adding concentrates or grain supplements, introduce them gradually rather than making a sudden change.

Which would help you most with that match—a hay test or sorting out the needs of different cattle? Either is a practical place to start.
