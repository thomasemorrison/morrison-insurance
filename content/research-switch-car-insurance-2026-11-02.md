New coverage should be documented before the old policy is canceled, and the two policies' actual effective dates and times must meet without a gap. ## Qualified facts used in the article

**A quote is not active coverage.** Progressive describes getting a quote, purchasing the policy, and activating coverage as distinct steps. It says immediate coverage typically follows processed payment when an immediate start is selected, but some insurers do not offer same-day coverage in every situation. The article does not promise instant issuance or treat a payment receipt as sufficient proof. A purchased future-effective policy is not covering the driver before its start. [7]

**Written confirmation comes before the old cancellation.** TDI's shopping guide expressly says not to cancel the current policy until receiving the new policy or a written statement that proves coverage. Progressive's switching guide says the new policy should already be effective before the old policy ends. A future-effective policy must be coordinated so the old coverage continues through the handoff. [4] [3]

**Confirm the time as well as the date.** Progressive specifically recommends asking the exact day and time a policy takes effect. The article's November 10, 12:01 a.m.-to-noon example is explicitly hypothetical, not a claim that all Texas policies start or end at midnight. The time-zone question is a prudent practical check, not a universal statutory requirement. [7]

**Renewal is not the only switching point.** TDI's 2026 Personal Automobile Insurance Consumer Bill of Rights, item 18, says consumers may cancel at any time and get a refund of unearned premium. Item 19 covers cancellation by either the insured or insurer. The article advises asking about scheduled renewal processing and any other-policy discount impact rather than asserting a universal automatic renewal procedure or guaranteed savings. [5]

**Finance and lease requirements need a separate check.** TDI says a lender requires collision and comprehensive if money is still owed on the vehicle. Progressive likewise says lenders or leasing companies typically require physical-damage coverage. Limits, deductible restrictions, listing requirements, and proof delivery are presented as questions to check in the reader's actual agreement, not uniform Texas mandates. [1] [6]

**Cancel through the company's process.** Progressive says procedures vary: some accept phone or online requests; some require written notification or signed forms. Its switching guide says previous-policy cancellation is typically the consumer's responsibility. The article does not promise Morrison or a replacement carrier automatically cancels the old policy. [3] [6]

**Stopping automatic payments is not the cancellation request.** The article separates formal cancellation from payment authorization management. TDI identifies nonpayment as a ground for insurer cancellation, and carrier guidance describes affirmative cancellation procedures. The article does not say stopping payment can never eventually result in cancellation; it explains why it is not a controlled substitute for requesting cancellation with the intended effective time. No universal collection, reinstatement, or credit-reporting consequence is asserted. [1] [2] [6]

**Unearned-premium refund rights apply to insured-requested cancellation too.** This scope was verified rather than inferred from the insurer-cancellation headline. TDI's auto guide expressly says “If either you or the company cancels your policy.” Its current 2026 Consumer Bill of Rights items 18–19 likewise expressly include the consumer's right to cancel and cancellation by either party. Unearned premium means money paid ahead that did not go toward coverage; it is not every premium dollar ever paid. [1] [5]

**No guaranteed refund amount or universal fee.** Progressive's general switching/cancellation guidance mentions potential cancellation fees and notes that state and timing affect them. This is not evidence that every Texas insurer may charge the same fee, or that any particular charge is lawful. The article therefore requests an itemized final statement and the basis for applicable charges, does not promise a check, and does not prescribe a universal prorated/short-rate calculation. Statutory unearned-premium rights are not described as wholly discretionary. [3] [6] [5]

## Refund deadline discrepancy and the drafting decision

TDI's short auto guide and requested cancellation tips say “within 15 days after the date of the cancellation.” The linked current **2026 Personal Automobile Insurance Consumer Bill of Rights**, item 19, provides more precise wording: **within 15 business days from the date the company receives notice of cancellation or the date of cancellation, whichever is later**. Its scope expressly includes cancellation by either the consumer or company. [1] [2] [5]

The public article intentionally does **not** state a universal numerical deadline; it explains the unearned-premium right and directs the reader to request final billing details. If a deadline is later added, use the current Bill of Rights' precise wording rather than converting the tips page into an unconditional 15-calendar-day promise. This handoff records the discrepancy instead of silently mixing the two formulations.
