## Research findings and controls

The strongest confirmed local anchors for this week are GivingTuesday on Tuesday, December 1; Fannie Brown Booth Memorial Library preschool storytime on Wednesday, December 2, 10:30–11:30 a.m. in the Redditt Room; the Shelby County Chamber’s Annual Taste of the Holidays on Thursday, December 3, at the John D. Windham Civic Center; and the Chamber’s Santa’s Lighted Christmas Parade on Saturday, December 5, downtown on the historic Center Square. The Chamber page gives the Taste date, venue, $15 advance-ticket/no-door-ticket detail, and the parade date and downtown location, but it does not publish a start time on the page. Treat parade time, route, road closures, theme, ticket availability, vendors, and weather plans as publication-day confirmation items. [1] [2]

GivingTuesday’s official participation page identifies December 1, 2026, and describes the day as a global generosity movement. It does not identify a Shelby County campaign, beneficiary, donation drive, or local need. Therefore the calendar may invite a neighbor to choose a verified local organization or act of service, but it must not name a local recipient or claim a current need until that organization confirms it directly. [3]

The library’s official event listing confirms the December 2 preschool storytime, the Redditt Room location, the 10:30–11:30 a.m. listing, and an age range of 3–5. The page also says the recurring description is Wednesday storytime and identifies the library’s official hours and location. Reconfirm the event page, cancellation status, and photo permissions on the day of use. Do not publish a child’s image without written permission. [4]

The useful household-safety anchor is Ready.gov’s power-outage guidance. It recommends keeping refrigerators and freezers closed, using generators only outdoors and at least 20 feet from windows, doors, and attached garages, keeping generators dry, and never using a gas stove or oven to heat a home. These are general preparedness points, not evidence of a local outage or forecast. [5]

For holiday travel, TxDOT’s official Holiday Season campaign says to arrange a sober ride ahead of time if drinking may be part of the evening and to encourage someone who has been drinking to find a sober ride or stay put. The page mentions a statewide campaign and a later heightened enforcement period; do not turn that into a Center-specific enforcement claim. [6]

Outdoor content needs restraint. TPWD’s 2026–27 dates page lists statewide or zone dates, but Shelby County applicability still requires the current county regulations page, species, zone, license, and any applicable special rules. Do not state that a particular species is open in Shelby County from the statewide dates page alone. [7] TPWD’s Toledo Bend report page currently says weekly reports are on hold while the agency improves reporting quality. Accordingly, use a lake-life stewardship or “check the current official source before you go” concept, not a current bite, water-level, species, or launch-condition claim. [8]

No local December event beyond the Chamber and library anchors is treated as confirmed in this report. City calendars, library listings, utility notices, county notices, weather alerts, road conditions, burn-ban status, hunting rules, and local organization needs must be checked again immediately before any related item is approved. The source-controls file specifically requires same-day rechecking of time-sensitive facts and prohibits invented event times, routes, closures, charity needs, weather claims, hunting rules, and school information. [9]
