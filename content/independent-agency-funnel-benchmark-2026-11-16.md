# Independent-agency funnel benchmark for Morrison Insurance

Independent agencies beyond Shelby County have built useful funnels that Morrison can examine. The strongest transferable examples are Berry Insurance’s education-to-conversation sequence, Crowley’s question-led resource with a named human next step, and Paradiso’s explicit service commitments. They show how original agency knowledge and a visible human process can lead a visitor toward an inquiry. **They do not establish that these current funnels consistently produce qualified leads.** Only Berry has a named-agency business-outcome case study in the supplied findings, and its vendor-published figures are self-reported and definition-limited.[1] [6] [8] [12]

These five examples are locally rooted independent agencies outside Shelby County, Texas, with personal or commercial property-and-casualty offerings. They are not captive carriers, lead aggregators, or national brokers. Their publicly stated identities make them relevant pattern references, not a matched performance peer group. The underlying map was checked on November 16, 2026; this report synthesizes those findings without new agency research or form submissions. Gordon Atlantic was excluded because its proposed domain resolved to a domain-for-sale page, preventing confirmation of a current operating agency URL.

Morrison’s immediate question is not whether to build a larger content library. Its latest user-reported Google Search Console snapshot is 1 click from 643 impressions across 28 days, with the actual date range unknown. The supplied social findings show 21 Facebook feed posts from October 19–November 15 producing 5 link clicks and median reach of 46; 21 Instagram feed posts had median reach of 9. These are Morrison context, not competitor-comparable metrics. They support a small, attributable test of the route from useful information to a real conversation—not a claim that more publishing, a new widget, or a full rebuild will solve acquisition.

## Berry Insurance: useful preparation before a human conversation

[Berry Insurance](https://www.berryinsurance.com/) presents a long-standing Franklin, Massachusetts agency with multi-carrier personal and business coverage. Its local history and education-led process give a prospect a reason to trust the people behind the next step. That is organizational proof, not acquisition proof.[1]

The concrete sequence is **preparation resource → education and service context → quote request → team contact**. The exact entry resource is its [Personal Insurance Worksheet](https://www.berryinsurance.com/hubfs/Forms/Personal_Insurance_Worksheet_Fillable.pdf), a four-page fillable document updated February 1, 2022. It prepares visitors for contact, coverage-history, home and auto questions, and says the agency can review it together on a call if the prospect gets stuck.[2] The [Insurance Learning Center](https://www.berryinsurance.com/insurance-resources?hsLang=en) adds articles, documents, podcasts and videos, including specific questions such as driver assignments.[3] The [Personal Insurance page](https://www.berryinsurance.com/personal-insurance) then explains service categories and routes to Get a Quote, describing a conversation about priorities, carrier options and customer choice.[4]

The [Contact page](https://www.berryinsurance.com/contact) makes the human route unusually visible: phone and text, email, chat and video appointments. It states that a team member will respond to a short form within 24 hours and lists weekday hours. That is a public promise; the map did not establish actual response performance, ownership of submissions or notification delivery.[5]

Berry has the strongest outcome evidence in this set, but it remains a vendor-published agency claim. [IMPACT’s Berry success story](https://www.impactplus.com/endless-customers-coaching/success-stories/berry-insurance) reports headline increases of 183% in business growth, 99% in leads and 69% in close rate without a clear headline denominator or comparison period. The same source gives agency-reported calendar-year figures: about 127 website form submissions and a 16% close rate in 2020, versus 253 submissions and a 27% close rate in 2021. Leadership also reported about $66K versus $185K in website-attributed written business; the source does not define whether those amounts are premium, revenue or another business measure. It provides no independent audit, qualification definition, attribution model or causal test. These figures justify examining the sequence, not setting Morrison targets from it.[6]

**What Morrison should take:** give a visitor one genuinely useful preparation aid beside a plain-language service page, explain what the first conversation covers, and preserve the visitor’s choice. Original local knowledge and a real team route matter more than the number of resource formats.

**What not to copy:** a long, personally sensitive worksheet as a mandatory first step; a sprawling library with 97 pages; repeated right-price or no-buyer’s-remorse framing; or a many-channel service promise that Morrison cannot staff and handle privately. Do not add chat simply because Berry has it, borrow testimonial-style reassurance, or treat the case-study percentages as expected results. The quote fields and post-submission screen were not exposed in the map, so neither ease of completion nor backend effectiveness is established.

## Crowley Insurance: answer a question, then introduce the person

[Crowley Insurance Agency](https://www.crowleyinsurance.com/) is an independent East Syracuse, New York agency whose About page describes a 1952 founding and three-generation family history. A named team and local office support a human, relationship-led presentation rather than an anonymous quote engine.[7]

Its sequence is **question-led education → family and team proof → initial request → further information and human review**. The exact education entry is [Coverage with Crowley 101](https://www.crowleyinsurance.com/coveragewithcrowley/), a large FAQ and video resource covering personal and business questions. It links to Start the Process with Mike, connecting learning to a person rather than leaving the reader inside a content library.[8]

The [Custom Insurance Proposal page](https://www.crowleyinsurance.com/new-quote-page/) explains that an initial form will open another page containing videos and longer forms needed to finalize a proposal.[9] The [Start the Process page](https://www.crowleyinsurance.com/nextstep/) asks for contact details, address, referral source, insurance type and current-insurance status. It includes text-message acceptance and an optional document upload, with persistent phone and email alternatives. No form was submitted, and the public pages did not establish an assigned responder or response-time expectation.[10]

The strongest transferable element is **the bridge from a specific question to a named human**, not the length of the form. Morrison can use a small locally relevant education piece and explain who reviews the request. This gives a prospect a reason to start a conversation without pretending the website can settle coverage suitability.

Crowley’s testimonials describe responsive service, including one account of a Friday quote request followed by insurance the next Tuesday. That is an undated, self-published anecdote, not a typical turnaround measure or conversion study. The supplied search findings did not locate a dated, denominator-based named-agency outcome report.[9] [11]

**What not to copy:** duplicated quote routes, vague language about another page popping up, or an unexplained upload before a human conversation. A first inquiry does not need full policy documents or a postal address unless Morrison identifies a genuine operational need. Do not promise the best coverage or price, a quick response without a defined standard, or that submitting a form guarantees a proposal. Permission to text should be understandable and should not remove a call or email alternative.

## Paradiso Insurance: make the service experience concrete

[Paradiso Insurance](https://www.paradisoinsurance.com/) identifies as a local independent agency in Stafford Springs, Connecticut, with personal and commercial offerings, a named team and community involvement. Its useful distinction is the specificity of the service process—not its app, social presence or number of quote tools.[12]

The mapped sequence is **service-promise video → written commitments → commercial review or quote entry → contact**. The named entry resource is [Our Paradiso Promise](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0HjuVBxsy_w), an approximately 11-year-old agency video with about 1.7K public views at the map check. YouTube presented a sign-in/bot barrier, so only public metadata and the description were observed. Neither video content nor view-to-inquiry conversion was verified.[16]

The [Paradiso Promise page](https://www.paradisoinsurance.com/paradiso-promise/) is more useful for Morrison than the video metric. Its 12-point commercial-lines commitment describes listening, explaining options, owning mistakes, setting next steps and time frames, and communicating through email or text. It also gives owner Chris Paradiso’s email for feedback.[13] The [Commercial Insurance page](https://www.paradisoinsurance.com/coverage/commercial-insurance/) describes a renewal-stage questionnaire and says the agency will contact the visitor if its review identifies coverage gaps or policy changes, explain the findings, and choose a course of action together. That is a concrete conditional expectation, not a guaranteed response time.[14]

The [Contact page](https://www.paradisoinsurance.com/contact/) offers phone, text, office and social routes. However, the path to a quote is fragmented: the header links to Jotform, the homepage/contact route to Canopy, and the commercial CTA to quoteparadiso.com. The public pages do not explain how those routes differ or when general inquiries receive a human response.[15]

**What Morrison should take:** a short service charter explaining how the local team listens, clarifies a question, sets the next step and owns follow-up. This is a useful way to differentiate service experience without attacking competitors or imitating a captive carrier’s product pitch. Any charter must describe Morrison’s actual process, not an aspirational guarantee.

**What not to copy:** three unexplained quote destinations, evening/weekend accommodation or time commitments without staffing evidence, an old video presented as current proof, or an app/portal in place of a person. No accessible, dated, denominator-based Paradiso funnel outcome was found in the supplied findings. Public views, audience counts and testimonial savings claims do not establish leads or conversions.

## The Murray Group: explain a risk, then explain the handoff

[The Murray Group](https://murraygrp.com/) identifies as an independent Latham, New York agency serving a defined Capital Region footprint. Its homepage organizes the offer into personal, business, employee benefits and private-client services, with carrier names and contact routes. These are useful orientation and identity signals, not proof of sales performance.[17]

The sequence is **service orientation → interactive risk education → experience proof → short request → risk-advisor review**. The [Business Insurance page](https://murraygrp.com/business-insurance/) uses the exact named resource Discover Clickable Coverage®, an embedded interactive graphic system. It explains risks across topics such as business owners insurance, commercial property, workers’ compensation and cyber liability, then repeats phone/contact routes. The extraction exposed the resource label but no separate public asset URL.[18]

The [Quote page](https://murraygrp.com/get-insurance-quote/) has the clearest general handoff explanation in the set: a risk advisor will reach out to review the submitted information and discuss an insurance solution, with no obligation. Visible fields include name, email, phone, insurance type, state and a help request.[19] The [Contact page](https://murraygrp.com/contact/) offers phone and an inquiry form and likewise says an expert will be in touch. Neither page gives a response-time standard or demonstrates backend behavior.[20]

Morrison should borrow the explanation of what a person does after the request. It does not need a licensed interactive widget to achieve that. A short, accurate local explanation can connect a homeowner’s or business owner’s question to the first conversation just as directly.

**What not to copy:** broad generic coverage menus, a consent-heavy initial form without plain-language privacy context, or undated testimonials as causal evidence. The extracted page material also contained template text reading Hello Producer and a malformed-looking Personal Graphics link; whether those artifacts are visible to an ordinary visitor was not established. They do not prove broken lead delivery. Testimonials and Google-review links provide anecdotal experience proof; no useful attributable lead or conversion study was found. [17] [21]

## McGhee Insurance: concrete scenarios with a direct-contact alternative

[The McGhee Insurance Agency](https://www.mcgheeins.com/) describes itself as independent and family-owned, operating in Arkansas since 1999, with North Little Rock and Fayetteville offices and licensing in Arkansas and Texas. The stated Texas relevance helps frame why the pattern is worth examining, but it does not make its results comparable to Morrison’s.[22]

Its sequence is **risk scenario → relevant service page → request → review options with the agency**, with direct contact available alongside the form. The exact resource is [Clickable Coverage®](https://www.mcgheeins.com/clickable-coverage/). Home-view hotspots explain scenarios such as fire, flood, water damage, liability and valuable possessions, and link toward home-insurance service content.[23] The [Home Insurance page](https://www.mcgheeins.com/personal-insurance/home-insurance/) translates the education into a discussion about individual risks and options, rather than displaying a self-serve price.[24]

The [Request Quote page](https://www.mcgheeins.com/request-quote/) describes three steps: contact the agency, review options together, and obtain coverage. Its form asks for name, email, phone, referral source, county and how the agency can help. It explicitly warns visitors not to include sensitive or private information and offers a direct-contact alternative.[25] The [Contact page](https://www.mcgheeins.com/contact/) supplies office locations and hours, phone, a shared text number and email. It does not identify an assigned responder or a response-time expectation.[26]

Morrison can use concrete local questions and the privacy warning without reproducing the widget or implying that every inquiry ends in coverage. The human discussion should be the clear next step, subject to the visitor’s needs and the agency’s ability to help.

**What not to copy:** the repeated long form and three-step blocks across education, service and contact pages; vague follow-up ownership; or a guaranteed-quote interpretation of the request copy. No dated, attributable funnel outcome with a defined goal and denominator was found in the supplied findings. Reviews, video presence and social follows are not traffic, qualified leads or conversion evidence.

## The small Morrison pilot worth proposing

The best combination is **Berry’s useful preparation, Crowley’s named-person bridge, and Paradiso’s concrete service expectations**. Murray contributes a useful sentence explaining the handoff; McGhee contributes a useful sensitive-information warning. None requires Morrison to rebuild its website, commission a large library or add software.

The following is an illustrative adaptation, not evidence that home/rural demand exceeds auto demand and not a change to the existing measurement-first priority. Verify inquiry delivery and attribution, then choose the actual visitor need using current evidence. Refresh an existing close-match page before considering another URL.

One compact example would be an ungated section on an existing relevant service page titled **“What to expect from a Morrison home and rural-property conversation.”** Address Center, Shelby County and the Piney Woods in Morrison’s own voice. Use Morrison’s approved 1923 history and confirmed local team information. Put a helpful first-conversation explanation ahead of product claims.

The page should answer a few practical questions: what the visitor hopes to clarify, the property’s general use, and what information may help a later discussion. It can explain that an outbuilding or mixed property use is something to discuss with an advisor without asserting that a particular policy covers it. Keep any preparation checklist on the page and optional. Do not ask visitors to upload declarations, driver information, account details or other sensitive documents at initial contact.

Place one clear primary next step beside the content, such as “Talk with a Morrison team member,” using a verified existing phone route. Offer an existing email or short contact route as an alternative. Add text only if its channel, consent and handling are confirmed. If a form is used, start with a name, usable contact method, brief service question and channel preference; explain not to include sensitive information. The copy should identify the actual team or responsible role, say that a person reviews the request, and explain the next conversation. Publish a response-time expectation only after Morrison confirms it can honor it. Do not make a price, coverage, claim or turnaround guarantee.

**One goal:** completed qualified new-business first conversations attributable to this page during a proposed 28-day observation window after an approved, verified public release. For this test, count a conversation only when a real prospect has an in-scope need, a licensed Morrison team member has actually spoken or exchanged substantive messages with them, and the page source is recorded. Exclude spam, duplicates and existing-customer service requests. Define the in-scope service area and business lines with Morrison before launch. Form submissions and button clicks are intermediate signals, not the goal, and no numerical conversion target is justified by the present evidence.

Before approval, the experiment needs three kinds of evidence. **Source evidence** means Morrison’s approved identity/history and service material, verified contact routes, and authoritative policy/carrier or regulator support for any insurance explanation. **Reviewer evidence** means a licensed Morrison reviewer checking insurance accuracy and suitability, plus the responsible staff member confirming ownership, privacy handling, consent and the promised first step. Use original team material only with permission; do not invent customers, testimonials or local experiences.

**Tracking evidence** means confirmation of what the existing measurement setup can record. Propose a stable page identifier and attributable website/contact-link events, with no personal information in event payloads. Use campaign tags only on approved inbound promotion links, not internal navigation. A staff record should connect the page source to the completed conversation, distinguish new business from service, and record response timing for internal learning. Any form test or event configuration needs separate authorization. Until measurement is approved and verified, do not call a click a delivered lead or claim that submissions reached the team.

At the end of the observation window, read the result in order: did relevant visitors arrive, did they attempt contact, and did a qualified conversation occur? If there were no such conversations, low exposure or missing attribution may make the test inconclusive. If contact attempts occurred but no human conversation followed, investigate the specific handoff rather than commissioning more content. Any later qualification or sale should retain its own definition and period; the first pilot must not inflate that into a revenue claim.

This is a proposal only. It authorizes no website, measurement or Google Business Profile changes; no paid media; no outreach, customer referrals, review solicitation or partner contacts; and no new recurring workflow. Any eventual promotion must pass existing approval gates and remain inside the Unified Content Desk, the sole workflow. Preserve the existing 7–10 weekly Facebook posts and 7–10 weekly Instagram posts separately, with the two-post-per-channel daily cap. This benchmark does not recommend specific social dates or times because no fresh per-channel capacity was checked. The test should replace or refine an approved content opportunity, not add another publishing lane or more volume.
