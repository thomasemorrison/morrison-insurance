## Safety decision — active Shelby County outdoor-burning order

Shelby County’s October 23 order remains the current county notice. It prohibits outdoor burning in the county’s unincorporated areas, except for activities carried out by Federal Certified Agencies. The declaration lasts no more than seven days unless the Commissioners Court continues or renews it. The Texas A&M Forest Service’s latest list and Shelby County Today both continue to show Shelby County’s burn ban as active. [1] [2] [3]

The point-alert feed returned no active alerts but retains an older embedded timestamp, so it is not used as a current all-clear. No newer county order, renewal, lift, evacuation, broad road closure, or weather warning was verified. The county order is the material safety item for today. [4]

## Toledo Bend lake note — current conditions, not a bite forecast

The Texas Water Development Board’s October 28 reading puts Toledo Bend at **84.4% full**, with a mean water level of **167.76 feet**. Its most recent instantaneous reading was **167.73 feet**, or **4.27 feet below** the 172-foot conservation-pool elevation; the agency labels these data provisional. [5]

The Sabine River Authority of Louisiana lists both generators as **No Run** and the spillway gates as **Closed**. Its site only calls out a road and recreation-area closure below the spillway when gates are open. [6]

A current public, lakewide fishing report is not available: TPWD states that weekly fishing reports are on hold while it improves the quality and accuracy of future reports, and its most recent visible Toledo Bend report is dated October 22, 2025. Do not present an old technique, species call, or water-temperature note as current information. [7]
