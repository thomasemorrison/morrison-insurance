# Wednesday Content Desk — Safety Scan
**Area:** Shelby County, Texas  
**Date:** Wednesday, September 23, 2026  
**Status:** Material alert confirmed — active burn ban

## What is confirmed

Shelby County’s September 17 order prohibits **all outdoor burning in the unincorporated areas of Shelby County**. The order contains an exception for outdoor-burning activities carried out by Federal Certified Agencies. The official county homepage continued to display “BURN BAN IN EFFECT” on September 23, and the Texas A&M Forest Service feed dated September 22 also listed Shelby County among counties under burn ban. [1] [2] [3]

No current National Weather Service watch, warning, or advisory was returned for forecast zone TXZ153 at the check time. The county forecast was hot but otherwise routine; that heat is not a separate material alert for this decision. [4] [5]

## Required scope and validity limits

This post must say **unincorporated Shelby County**. Do not describe it as a city-wide restriction and do not imply that it establishes restrictions within incorporated municipalities. Do not claim there is an active wildfire, evacuation, flood emergency, broad road closure, or weather warning; none was confirmed in the reviewed sources.

The order says the local disaster declaration continues for no more than seven days unless Shelby County Commissioners Court continues or renews it. Because the order was posted/effective September 17, its initial term raises a renewal or expiry checkpoint around **September 24**. A September 23 court agenda included ratification of the burn ban, but the reviewed material did not establish the meeting outcome or provide a separate renewal instrument. The county’s current “in effect” posting and the Forest Service listing support this September 23 post; recheck the county source before using this message after September 23. [1] [2] [3] [6]
