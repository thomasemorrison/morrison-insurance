**Source documents:** Faceless Instagram Content Strategy, Instagram Opening Hooks, Home Insurance Retention Script, Instagram Growth Strategy, Brand Positioning (Teen Drivers)
**Last revised:** July 2026
**Platforms:** Facebook · Instagram
**Tone:** Trusted neighbor. Warm, plain-spoken, locally grounded. Never salesy, never corporate.

---

## How This Plan Fits the Existing Calendar

The existing weekly rhythm (Monday community post, Wednesday automated blog, Friday brand/seasonal) stays intact. This plan adds depth to the **Friday** slot and introduces a new **Instagram-native series** designed for saves, shares, and follower growth — the metrics that drive organic reach on Instagram in 2026.

The five source documents collectively point to the same strategy: position Morrison as a **localized lifestyle and community hub that happens to sell insurance**, not an insurance page trying to force community engagement. Every post below is built on that principle.

---

## What to Avoid (from the Growth Strategy document)

Before building the schedule, these seven patterns should be actively avoided — they dominate the insurance industry on Instagram and consistently underperform:

| Pattern | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| "What is [Coverage]?" definition graphics | Reads like a textbook. People don't use Instagram to study. |
| Fear-mongering scenarios | Creates anxiety, people scroll past to avoid it. |
| Generic "Get a Quote Today" posts | Zero value. Pure sales pitch. |
| Agent desk selfies with coffee | Slightly human but not shareable or useful. |
| Corporate motivational quotes | Overused, impersonal, indistinguishable from competitors. |
| Carrier-centric bragging | Customers care about their experience, not your carrier roster. |
| Forced trending audio dances | Rarely converts casual viewers into local followers. |

### Series 1 — The First Truck Reality Check *(Teen Auto)*
**Format:** 4-slide carousel
**Pillar:** Seasonal Tips / Teen Audience
**Target:** East Texas teens 15–20, parents of teen drivers
**Algorithm goal:** Saves + tags

This series speaks directly to the East Texas teen experience — trucks, backroads, pine trees, and the reality of paying for insurance for the first time. The tone is the "Direct" version from the Brand Positioning document: no jargon, no lecture, just practical help from a neighbor.

**Slide structure:**
- Slide 1 — Hook: "POV: You finally got the truck..."
- Slide 2 — Twist: "...but now you have to pay for the insurance."
- Slide 3 — Value: "3 ways to not go broke driving in East Texas:"
- Slide 4 — Payoff: "1. Good grades actually pay off — ask about the discount. 2. Keep your record clean on those backroads. 3. Let a local agent do the heavy lifting."

**Caption (brand voice adjusted):**
Getting the keys is the best part. Paying for the insurance? Not so much. But there are actually ways to lower your rate before you even turn 21 — good grades, a clean record, and having a local team who picks up the phone when you call. We're not a 1-800 number. We're East Texas. Save this for when you're ready to get insured, and tag a friend who just got their license. 🛻🌲

**Visual direction:**
- Slide 1: Cinematic pickup truck on a pine-lined dirt road, golden hour, no people
- Slide 2: Close-up of keys on a worn wooden fence post or truck tailgate
- Slide 3: Driver's-perspective shot of a two-lane blacktop through the Piney Woods — steering wheel visible, no driver
- Slide 4: Clean text-forward slide, blurred forest background, brand colors

### Series 4 — Home Insurance Reality Check *(Homeowner Reel/Carousel)*
**Format:** Reel with text overlays OR carousel caption
**Pillar:** Seasonal Tips
**Target:** East Texas homeowners
**Algorithm goal:** Saves + shares (tag a spouse trigger)

This is the strongest single piece of content in the five documents. The hook is a proven pattern interrupt. Every line earns the next. The three-point checklist drives saves.

**Hook:** "Your home survived the last storm. Your policy might not."

**Full script (use as Reel voiceover, carousel caption, or standalone caption):**
Your home survived the last storm. Your policy might not.

Most East Texas homeowners set up their coverage years ago — and never looked back. But your home's value has gone up. Lumber costs have doubled. Labor isn't cheap anymore.

That policy from five years ago? It might rebuild half your house.

Here's what to check right now:

1. Your dwelling coverage limit. Does it match what it would actually cost to rebuild — not what you paid for the house?
2. Your deductible. Some policies have a separate wind and hail deductible. East Texas gets both. A lot.
3. Your personal property coverage. Walk through one room. Add up what's in it. Most people are shocked.

You don't need to figure this out alone. That's exactly what a local, independent agent is for — someone who knows this area, knows these storms, and works for you, not the insurance company.

If you haven't had a policy review in the last two years, it's time. No pressure. Just a conversation.

📍 Center, TX | Serving Shelby County and Deep East Texas since 1923.

## Brand Voice Quick Reference

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "We're your neighbors. We live here too." | "Get a free quote today!" |
| Soft CTA: "No pressure. Just a conversation." | Hard CTA: "Call us now before it's too late!" |
| Specific and local: "East Texas storms," "Toledo Bend," "Shelby County" | Generic: "Protect your family" |
| Plain English: "The part you pay before we help" | Jargon: "Your deductible obligation" |
