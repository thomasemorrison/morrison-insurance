### Hook
Most homeowners assume their home insurance covers everything inside the house. It covers a lot — but not everything, and not all of it at full value. If you have firearms, jewelry, or inherited items in your home, there's a good chance you have a gap you don't know about.

### Core Concept
Standard home insurance policies include personal property coverage, but that coverage has **sub-limits** for specific categories. Firearms, jewelry, silverware, and collectibles are common examples. A policy might cover personal property up to $100,000 total — but only up to $2,500 for firearms and $1,500 for jewelry, regardless of actual value.

A **scheduled personal property endorsement** (also called a floater or rider) solves this by listing specific high-value items individually, insuring them at their appraised or documented value, and often covering them for risks a standard policy doesn't — including mysterious disappearance (like losing an earring at the county fair).

### Sections to Cover

**1. The Sub-Limit Problem**
Explain how standard HI policies work and why sub-limits exist. Use specific, relatable examples:
- A gun safe with $8,000 in rifles and handguns — only $2,500 is covered at a standard limit
- An engagement ring worth $4,500 — standard jewelry sub-limit may be $1,500
- A grandmother's pearl necklace with sentimental and appraised value — if it's not documented, the claim is a guess

**2. Firearms in East Texas Homes**
This is especially relevant locally. Many East Texas families have multiple firearms — hunting rifles, shotguns, handguns — accumulated over generations. In a fire or a theft, the total value can easily exceed standard sub-limits. A scheduled endorsement covers each firearm at its documented value. Encourage readers to:
- Make a list of every firearm with make, model, and serial number
- Keep photos and receipts (or a written appraisal for older or collectible pieces)
- Store that documentation somewhere other than the home (cloud backup, safe deposit box)

**3. Jewelry — More Common Than People Think**
Not just engagement rings. Consider:
- Diamond earrings, anniversary gifts, family heirlooms
- Items worn regularly (higher risk of loss or damage)
- Items that leave the house — county fair, church, travel

The county fair earring example is perfect here: a standard HI policy typically does not cover mysterious disappearance (you lost it — no theft, no fire, no covered peril). A scheduled jewelry floater usually does.

**4. Inherited and Specialty Items**
This is the most overlooked category. Inherited items often have:
- No receipt or purchase record
- Sentimental value that exceeds market value (or vice versa)
- No documentation at all

Encourage readers to get appraisals for inherited jewelry, art, collectibles, firearms, and silverware. An appraisal creates a paper trail and gives the insurance company a number to work from.

**5. How to Get It Done**
Simple three-step close:
1. Make a list of anything in your home worth more than $500 that you'd be upset to lose
2. Take photos and gather any receipts, appraisals, or serial numbers
3. Call Morrison Insurance — we'll tell you exactly what your current policy covers and what it doesn't
