### Vehicle-by-Vehicle Breakdown

**ATVs and UTVs (Side-by-Sides)**
These are everywhere in East Texas — used for hunting, ranching, recreation, and getting around the property. They are almost never covered under a standard auto policy. A homeowner's policy may cover theft if the ATV is stored on your property, but it typically will not cover damage, liability, or loss away from home. A dedicated ATV/UTV policy covers:
- Collision and comprehensive (damage to the machine)
- Liability (if you injure someone or damage their property)
- Medical payments
- Uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage
- Accessories and custom equipment (winches, lift kits, light bars)

Key nuance: UTVs used on public roads in Texas may require a different type of coverage than those used only on private property. Worth a conversation.

**Motorcycles**
A motorcycle is not covered under your auto policy — it needs its own policy. Coverage options mirror auto: liability, collision, comprehensive, medical payments, and uninsured motorist. Riders should also consider:
- Custom parts and equipment coverage (aftermarket exhaust, custom paint, saddlebags)
- Roadside assistance
- Lay-up/storage discounts if the bike is only ridden seasonally

**Boats and Personal Watercraft**
Toledo Bend is in our backyard. A lot of East Texas families have a bass boat, a pontoon, or a jet ski — and a lot of them assume it's covered under their homeowner's policy. It may be, up to a point (typically $1,000–$1,500 for small boats), but that limit is almost never enough for a real boat. A dedicated watercraft policy covers:
- Physical damage (collision, fire, theft, sinking)
- Liability (if you injure someone or damage another vessel)
- Medical payments
- Towing and assistance on the water
- Trailer coverage

Key nuance: Coverage needs differ between a 14-foot aluminum fishing boat and a 24-foot bass boat. The value, the use, and the storage location all affect the right policy.

**Tractors and Farm Equipment**
A farm tractor is typically not covered under a standard homeowner's or auto policy. It may be covered under a farm and ranch policy — but only if that policy is written correctly and the equipment is listed. Key considerations:
- Tractors used only on private property vs. those driven on public roads (different coverage requirements)
- Implements and attachments (bush hogs, plows, loaders) — these need to be listed separately
- Older equipment vs. newer equipment — agreed value vs. ACV matters here just like it does for homes
- Liability if the tractor causes damage or injury off your property

**Riding Lawnmowers and Zero-Turns**
A homeowner's policy typically covers a riding mower for theft if it's stored on the property. It does not cover mechanical breakdown, and liability coverage is limited. For high-value zero-turns ($8,000–$15,000+), it's worth asking whether the coverage limit is adequate.

**Classic and Collector Cars**
This is one of the most underinsured categories in East Texas. A classic car insured under a standard auto policy is valued at ACV — which for a 40-year-old vehicle may be very low, even if the car has been fully restored and is worth significantly more. A classic car or agreed value policy:
- Insures the car at an agreed value (what you and the insurer agree it's worth at the time of the policy)
- Pays that full amount in the event of a total loss — no depreciation argument
- Often includes coverage for spare parts and memorabilia
- May have mileage restrictions or storage requirements

Key nuance: The difference between a standard auto policy and an agreed value collector car policy can be tens of thousands of dollars at claim time.

**Campers and RVs**
An RV or travel trailer is not covered under a standard auto policy. A dedicated RV policy covers the vehicle itself, personal belongings inside, liability, and often full-timer coverage for those who live in their RV. Fifth wheels and travel trailers need coverage even when they're parked — a homeowner's policy typically does not extend to them.

### The Bigger Picture

Most people don't think about insuring these items until one of them gets stolen, damaged, or involved in an accident. By then it's too late. The conversation is simple: make a list of everything you own that isn't your daily driver, and let's make sure it's covered correctly. Some of it may already be covered under policies you have. Some of it may not be. We'd rather find out now.
