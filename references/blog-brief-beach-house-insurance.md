### The Three Layers — Explained Simply

**Layer 1: Homeowner's Insurance (the base policy)**
A standard homeowner's policy covers the structure and personal property from most common perils — fire, theft, vandalism, and some weather events. But on the Texas coast, the standard market gets complicated fast. Many traditional insurance carriers either won't write coastal properties at all, or they write them with significant exclusions. Don't assume your current carrier will cover a second home on the coast. We'll quote it — but we may need to look at specialty coastal markets or surplus lines carriers to find coverage that works.

**Layer 2: Windstorm Insurance through TWIA**
This is the one that surprises most buyers. In the designated catastrophe areas of the Texas coast — which includes Galveston County and the Bolivar Peninsula — windstorm coverage is **not included in a standard homeowner's policy**. It must be purchased separately through the **Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA)**, the state's insurer of last resort for coastal wind and hail damage.

TWIA coverage is mandatory if you have a mortgage on the property. It is also strongly advisable even if you own it free and clear — because wind is the primary driver of catastrophic loss on the Texas coast. Hurricane Ike in 2008 devastated Bolivar Peninsula almost entirely through wind and storm surge. Without TWIA coverage, a major storm could mean a total loss with no payout.

Key points about TWIA:
- It is a separate policy with its own premium and deductible
- The deductible is typically a percentage of the insured value (e.g., 2–5%), not a flat dollar amount — on a $300,000 home, a 2% deductible is $6,000
- The property must be inspected and meet TWIA construction standards to qualify
- There are coverage limits — very high-value properties may need excess windstorm coverage on top of TWIA

**Layer 3: Flood Insurance through NFIP or Private Market**
Flood damage is **never covered by a standard homeowner's policy** — not on the coast, not anywhere. On the Texas coast, flood risk is significant and in many areas mandatory if the property is in a FEMA-designated Special Flood Hazard Area (which most coastal properties are).

Flood insurance is available through the **National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)**, administered by FEMA, or through private flood insurers who may offer broader coverage or higher limits. Key points:
- NFIP has coverage limits: $250,000 for the structure, $100,000 for contents
- Private flood insurance can offer higher limits and broader coverage
- There is typically a 30-day waiting period before NFIP coverage takes effect — you cannot buy it when a storm is already in the Gulf
- Flood insurance covers rising water. It does not cover storm surge in the same way windstorm covers wind damage — the distinction matters and can affect claims

### What to Do Before You Buy

Give readers a practical pre-purchase checklist:
1. **Ask about the flood zone.** Request the FEMA flood map designation for the property. If it's in Zone AE or VE, flood insurance is likely required and will be a significant annual cost.
2. **Ask about TWIA eligibility.** The property must meet TWIA construction standards. An older home or one that hasn't been updated may not qualify — or may require repairs before it can be insured.
3. **Budget for three separate premiums.** Homeowner's, TWIA windstorm, and flood are three separate policies with three separate premiums and three separate deductibles.
4. **Don't wait until closing.** Start the insurance conversation early — ideally before you make an offer. Some properties are difficult or expensive to insure, and that affects the value of the deal.
5. **Call us.** We will walk you through all of it. Even if we end up pointing you to a coastal specialist for the beach house itself, we are your agent for everything else — and we want to make sure you go into this with eyes open.
