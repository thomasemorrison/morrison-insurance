## Friday — Rural Ag Report

Prepare one **review-ready Rural Ag Report** within this same Content Desk—never as a new task or schedule. Write for Shelby County neighbors with a few feeder cattle, a small tract of trees, or a backyard garden. Keep cattle and timber coverage basic and high-level, not a commercial market bulletin. Choose one main useful topic and add a brief seasonal garden note when supported; do not force all three subjects into every report.

For cattle, answer the seller's question in plain language using the latest [USDA AMS Texas Weekly Cattle Auction Summary](https://mymarketnews.ams.usda.gov/viewReport/1955), with its covered week and a stated relevant comparison. Lead with a supported verdict such as “a softer week for sellers,” “about the same,” “looking better,” or “looking good,” then one short explanation. A weekly fall is not automatically a historically poor market; a rise is not automatically a strong market. Keep comparable cattle grades, weights, units and price rows in internal research, not public grids. State averages are not local offers; do not predict prices or recommend buying/selling.

For timber, answer “How are timber prices for a landowner selling trees?” using Texas A&M Forest Service resources and [Timber Price Trends](https://tfsweb.tamu.edu/forest-land/forest-industry-economics/timber-price-trends/). Name the covered period and comparison. Check standing-tree prices, not delivered-log prices, and distinguish pine sawtimber, chip-n-saw and pulpwood internally. If mixed, say so rather than calling all timber good or bad. “About the same for years” requires a verified comparable multi-year series; “looking good” needs favorable price-level evidence, not just one rise. Share real optimism when supported, but distinguish encouraging buyer/mill signals from demonstrated landowner-price recovery. Do not adopt a long-downturn claim without checking it, repeat old values as this week's prices, estimate tract value or recommend a timber harvest. If evidence is missing, say “No fresh price read yet” or omit the verdict; don't invent one. Use a separately supported garden/woodland topic rather than forcing a stale price story.

For gardening, use [Shelby County Extension](https://shelby.agrilife.org/), Texas A&M AgriLife regional/crop guidance and specialist fruit resources first. Include practical preparation, planting or harvest notes only after verifying the crop, season, local frost/soil conditions and any exact date. A USDA hardiness zone describes winter cold, not soil type or this week's planting date; do not assign all Shelby County properties one soil classification or a universal fertilizer/lime rate. The [Old Farmer's Almanac Center 75935 calendar](https://www.almanac.com/gardening/planting-calendar/zipcode/75935) is a supplemental frost-based guide, not a guarantee. Farmers' Almanac's nationwide moon calendar is optional labeled tradition, not proven local advice. Do not blindly apply generic Texas dates, confuse seeds/transplants, reproduce full copyrighted charts, or promise yields.
