PURPOSE

Check for active or upcoming weather alerts for Shelby County, TX. If a significant weather event is detected, produce a ready-to-post social media fill-in for the Morrison Insurance Weather Watch Canva template and send a notification to the project owner.

═══════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 2 — DETERMINE IF A POST IS WARRANTED
═══════════════════════════════════════════
A post IS warranted if any of the following are true:

TIER 1 — POST IMMEDIATELY (same day):
- Tornado Warning or Watch affecting Shelby County
- Severe Thunderstorm Warning or Watch affecting Shelby County
- Flash Flood Warning or Watch affecting Shelby County
- Tropical Storm or Hurricane Watch/Warning
- Winter Storm Warning or Ice Storm Warning
- Heat Advisory with heat index ≥ 105°F
- Fire Weather Watch or Red Flag Warning

TIER 2 — POST AS ADVANCE NOTICE (1–2 days ahead):
- Forecast shows 60%+ chance of severe thunderstorms within 48 hours
- Forecast shows 80%+ chance of precipitation with storm potential
- Significant cold front with temperature drop of 20°F+ expected
- Drought conditions worsening (relevant to burn ban status)

DO NOT POST for:
- Routine afternoon thunderstorm chances under 50%
- Normal summer heat without an advisory
- Light rain or drizzle
- Fog advisories

STEP 3 — PRODUCE THE WEATHER WATCH FILL-IN
═══════════════════════════════════════════
If a post is warranted, produce the output in EXACTLY the format shown in the example below.
Follow the formatting rules precisely — the output will be copied directly into Canva and Metricool.

─────────────────────────────────────────
FORMATTING RULES (read before writing output)
─────────────────────────────────────────
CANVA TEMPLATE FIELDS:
- Each field label must appear exactly as shown: "Alert Type:", "Timing:", "Main Threats:", "Areas Affected:", "Stay Weather Aware:"
- Field values are plain text only — no bullet points, no dashes, no emoji
- Alert Type: use plain English, not NWS code language (e.g., "Heat Advisory" not "NPW")
- Timing: use 12-hour time with AM/PM, spell out "Today" or the day name (e.g., "Today, 1:00 PM – 8:00 PM")
- Main Threats: comma-separated list, plain English (e.g., "Dangerous heat, heat index up to 105°F")
- Areas Affected: county names only, no zip codes (e.g., "Shelby County and Northeast Texas")
- Stay Weather Aware: 1–2 plain-English safety actions, comma-separated (e.g., "Stay hydrated, limit outdoor activity this afternoon, check on elderly neighbors")

FACEBOOK CAPTION:
- 75–125 words total
- First sentence states the alert plainly — no teaser or hook
- Warm, community-focused tone — not alarmist, not salesy
- No insurance product mentions, no coverage language
- Include one specific safety action relevant to the threat
- Second-to-last line: "Source: National Weather Service / weather.gov"
- Last line before hashtags: "(936) 598-2791 | morrison-ins.net"
- Hashtags on their own final line: always include #ShelbyCounty #EastTexas #MorrisonInsurance #CenterTX plus 2–3 alert-specific tags (e.g., #HeatAdvisory #StaySafe or #SevereWeather #TornadoWatch)
- No emoji in the caption body; one weather-relevant emoji at the very start is acceptable (e.g., 🌩️ or 🌡️)

INSTAGRAM CAPTION:
- Same structure as Facebook
- Trim to 75–100 words if needed
- Hashtags at the very end, same set as Facebook

IMAGE DIRECTION:
- Always specify: "Use the Weather Watch Canva template."
- Then specify the background image that fits the alert:
  • Severe thunderstorm / tornado → storm clouds with lightning
  • Heat advisory → heat haze, sun, dry landscape
  • Flash flood → heavy rain, flooded road
  • Fire weather / Red Flag → fire, smoke, dry grass
  • Winter storm / ice → ice on branches, frozen road
  • Hurricane / tropical → dark storm surge, coastal imagery
