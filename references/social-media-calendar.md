## Purpose and operating principle

Morrison Insurance should be **the useful, recognizable Shelby County account that happens to be an insurance agency**. The account earns attention through local service, familiar places, outdoor and rural life, and plain-language help. It earns an inquiry only after it has demonstrated that it understands the community.

> **Content test:** Would someone in Shelby County find this useful, recognizable, or worth passing to a neighbor even if they were not shopping for insurance today? If not, it does not lead the calendar.

This plan replaces the July 2026 two-post manual rotation. It aligns the Instagram audit with the active Weekday Content Desk, the weekly sports and fishing deliverables, current SEO work, and Morrison’s community-first brand requirements. It is a planning framework, not permission to post automatically.

## Direction carried forward from the P&C website benchmark

The social presence supports the same simple local journey as the website: **recognize a familiar situation → find useful local help → choose an easy human next step.** A post earns attention by being helpful, recognizable, or genuinely shareable first. It may then make a soft, relevant invitation to Morrison; it must not become a thinly disguised product pitch.

Prioritize real questions, local utility, familiar places, outdoor and rural life, school and community information, and calm preparation guidance. When insurance education is appropriate, frame it around what someone can gather, notice, or ask before speaking with an agent. Use ordinary local language such as “Bring your renewal” or “Let’s walk through the questions,” rather than savings promises, fear-based urgency, or corporate claims.

Creative must feel rooted in Center, Shelby County, or Deep East Texas. Prefer authentic Morrison, community-approved, school-provided, archival-with-permission, or recognizable local imagery. Keep a clear visual hierarchy and one central point. Never use stock handshakes, suit-forward or carrier-style graphics, a generic photo that could be anywhere, or an agency headshot as the whole message. The complete operating standard is preserved in the shared project file **Morrison Insurance — Website, Content, and Design Direction**.

### Timely homeowner question — Texas roof-age rule clarification

Treat the September 2026 TDI roof-age clarification as a **plain-language homeowner question**, not a political or premium-savings story. The useful question is: “Can a Texas insurer decline or nonrenew a home policy just because the roof is old?” TDI says age alone cannot be the basis, while the physical condition of the roof and other home components may still be considered. TDI’s proposed amendment says it clarifies an existing prohibition and does not change its meaning or application. Do not promise acceptance, renewal, coverage, a rate reduction, or a market-wide affordability outcome.

The preferred web direction is a focused, source-dated FAQ rather than a revision of the existing rate-increase article: **“Can a Texas Insurer Deny Home Insurance Because Your Roof Is Old? What the Rule Actually Says.”** It should distinguish roof age from roof condition, explain what a homeowner can gather when a notice arrives, state the limits clearly, and cite the TDI release and rulemaking document. Before publication, recheck the rulemaking status. A related feed post may take a useful article/education slot within the current 7–10-per-channel weekly mix only after the article is live, facts and image rights are refreshed, a fresh Metricool check confirms channel-specific daily room, and Morrison approves. Replace a lower-priority concept if needed rather than exceed the channel’s two-placement daily ceiling. ## Format, creative, and caption standards

| Format | Best use | Morrison creative standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Carousel** | Sports schedules, guides, checklists, event information, local history | Use 1080×1350 (4:5) images where possible. Lead with the outcome on slide one, use five to seven concise slides, and end with a practical checklist or one clear question. |
| **Original Reel** | Local discovery, personality, demonstrations, people and places | Use a real person or recognizable local setting in the first second. Keep it to one point in 15–35 seconds, add captions and large on-screen text, and finish with one easy response prompt. |
| **Single local photo** | Community recognition, history, local people, authentic scenes | Tell a short story about the person or place. Use permission-based photography or authentic agency/local imagery, not generic stock. |
| **Stories** | Polls, countdowns, reminders, live event context, questions | Use poll, slider, question-box, quiz, and countdown tools to learn what the audience wants. Save durable community resources into Highlights when appropriate. |
| **Collaborator post** | Local non-follower reach | Invite the featured organization, photographer, business, event organizer, or nonprofit only when it genuinely appears in the content. |

Captions should open with a local situation or useful point—not with “we” or a product pitch. Each permanent post needs **one low-friction response path**, such as a choice, memory, place name, tag, save, or share request. Avoid generic “What do you think?” prompts and repeated phone-number endings.

> **Useful response prompts:** “Bass, crappie, or catfish?”; “What was your first movie at the Rio?”; “Which road has you watching for deer at dusk?”; “Tag a parent who needs this week’s school schedule.”

Use a restrained, specific hashtag set rather than a long block. Start with the local place and subject in ordinary caption language, then use three to five relevant tags such as `#ShelbyCountyTX`, `#CenterTX`, `#DeepEastTexas`, `#ToledoBend`, and one subject-specific tag. Do not use a generic insurance tag merely to fill space.

## Thirty-day measurement and review

The account is small enough that a few interactions can create large percentage changes. Evaluate **series**, not a single post. For the next four weeks, log each post’s subject, format, reach, likes, comments, saves, shares, profile activity, follows, and any qualitative local response.

| Measure | Initial test objective | Decision use |
|---|---|---|
| **Comments** | At least one genuine comment on 25% of permanent feed posts | Identify prompts and subjects that create conversation. |
| **Saves and shares** | At least one save or share on half of guide/checklist carousels | Prioritize practical series worth keeping. |
| **Local participation** | Four photo submissions, poll responses, community tags, or collaborator interactions | Test whether the account is becoming a local conversation space. |
| **Reach and follows** | Establish a baseline by series, then compare weeks three and four | Keep the two series that consistently bring non-followers or follows. |
| **Conversion quality** | Track DMs, profile visits, website taps, and calls separately from public engagement | Do not mistake a low-comment service inquiry for a failed post, or a high-like post for a lead. |
